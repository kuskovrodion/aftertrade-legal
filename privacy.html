Privacy Policy

Your journal is a private financial record. This page describes what the app does with data — feature by feature, not in general terms.

Last updated 30 July 2026

The short version

  • Your journal never leaves your devices except through your own iCloud account.
  • Voice recording and AI processing happen on your device. Audio is never uploaded.
  • Usage analytics are off until you turn them on, and never contain journal content.
  • Crash reports are collected in the App Store build so malfunctions can be fixed.
  • No advertising, no tracking across other apps or websites, no sale of data.

1Who is responsible

Aftertrade is built and operated by an independent developer. For any privacy question or request, write to aftertradesupport@gmail.com.

2Your journal

What you enter stays yours: instrument, direction, entry and exit price, position size, stop loss, take-profit levels, realized profit and loss, timeframe, setups, mindsets, notes, and any chart image you attach. It is stored on your device.

If you have iCloud enabled, this data is mirrored to the private database of your own iCloud account so your devices stay in step. That copy lives in your Apple account, not on any server belonging to Aftertrade. The developer cannot read it, cannot access it, and holds no copy of it. Apple's handling of it is governed by Apple's Privacy Policy.

If you sign out of iCloud or disable it for Aftertrade, the journal simply stays local to the device.

3Voice logging

  • The microphone is used only while you are actively recording a trade, and only after you grant permission.
  • Speech is turned into text by Apple's on-device speech recognition, using models installed locally on your iPhone. The audio is processed on the device and is not sent to any server — not Apple's, and not ours.
  • The recording is not saved. It is discarded when the capture session ends; only the transcript you can see on screen is kept, and only long enough to build your draft entry.

4AI features

Turning your spoken words into a draft trade, and writing the short summaries you see in reviews, both use Apple's on-device foundation model. This runs locally on your iPhone. No third-party AI provider is involved, nothing is sent to a server for processing, and your journal is never used to train any model.

5Usage analytics — off unless you allow it

Aftertrade can measure which features get used, so the app can be improved. This is handled by Mixpanel, using their European ingestion endpoint.

  • Disabled by default. The app starts opted out, asks you once on first launch, and you can change your mind at any time in Profile → Privacy. If you decline, or later switch it off, nothing is sent.
  • What is sent: which screens were opened and which actions were taken, described as fixed event names, plus counts, yes/no flags, and fixed category values. Alongside them: the platform (ios), the app version, and the build number.
  • What is never sent: no instrument, price, position size, profit and loss, note text, chart link, or any other journal content. Events carry shape only — how many, whether, which category. This is enforced by the app's own code, not merely promised here.
  • No identity. There is no account, name, or email address to attach. The app never tells Mixpanel who you are; events carry only an anonymous per-installation identifier that Mixpanel generates.
  • Deriving a coarse location from your IP address is switched off.

Mixpanel processes this on our behalf under their privacy policy.

6Crash and performance reports

App Store builds include New Relic to report crashes and performance problems. It is not present in development builds. It collects crash reports, stability and performance metrics, and ordinary technical context such as device model, operating system version, and app version.

Two things are worth stating plainly:

  • Every network-capture feature is switched off before the reporter starts — URL logging, network request events, request error events, response body capture, and distributed tracing. This is deliberate: a chart link is journal content, and no diagnostic tool may record it.
  • This reporter starts when the app launches, separately from the analytics choice in section 5. It reports malfunctions rather than usage, and a crash on the launch path happens before any choice can be recorded. If this concerns you, write to the address above.

New Relic processes this under their privacy policy.

7Searching for an instrument

When you search for a coin, the text you typed is sent to CoinMarketCap's public API to find matching assets, and asset logos are loaded from their servers. Only the search text leaves your device — no API key, no identifier, and nothing from your journal. CoinMarketCap's handling is governed by their privacy policy.

8Chart attachments

If you attach a chart by pasting a link, the app requests that address directly from the host you named, over HTTPS only, without cookies or credentials. That host will see the request in its own server logs, exactly as if you had opened the link in a browser. Attach links you are comfortable requesting.

If you attach a chart from your photo library instead, the image is copied into your journal and is then covered by section 2. Aftertrade uses Apple's system picker, which hands over only the image you chose and gives the app no access to the rest of your library.

9Notifications

The app may ask for permission to send alerts so the capability is ready. Aftertrade currently sends no notifications — no marketing, no reminders, nothing. Separately, iCloud sync relies on Apple's silent notification mechanism to learn that another one of your devices changed something; that is Apple's own infrastructure and carries no journal content to us.

10What Aftertrade does not do

  • No advertising, ad networks, or advertising identifiers.
  • No tracking of you across other apps or websites.
  • No selling or sharing of data with data brokers.
  • No user accounts and no third-party sign-in.
  • No collection of your location.
  • No use of your journal for anything other than showing it back to you.

11How long data is kept

Your journal
Until you delete it. Deleting a trade removes it from the device and, when sync is on, from your iCloud. Deleting the app removes the local copy; the iCloud copy is managed in iOS Settings under your Apple Account.
Analytics events
Retained by Mixpanel under that project's retention settings.
Crash reports
Retained by New Relic under that account's retention settings.

12Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access, correct, export, or delete personal data held about you. Two things make this unusually simple here: your journal is already in your own possession and your own iCloud account, and analytics events carry no identifier that would let anyone tie them back to you. For anything else, write to the address above and it will be handled.

13Children

Aftertrade is not directed at children and is not intended for anyone under 13, or under the minimum age required in your country.

14Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, this page is updated and the date at the top changes with it. A change that affects what analytics collect would also be reflected in the app's own privacy settings.
